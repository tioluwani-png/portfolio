title: The Lie of a Thousand Options
date: 2026-10-09

summary: Why we’re scared of depth, and why some people can’t be replaced.
---

So I was talking to a friend recently about **Companionship**. What it really means, and what it means to love somebody.

I’m a firm believer that you need that isolation phase. You need to be alone, lock in, figure out who you are. But here’s the reality: I also think everyone needs a companion. Somebody you can talk to every day, tell about your day, laugh with, do things with.

And the moment people hear “**companionship**,” they think sex. It doesn’t have to be that. It can be the person you call about random things that happened today. The one you pray with, the one you can be completely free around. Not even romantic, although honestly those things usually lead to romance. It’s just somebody who sees you bare, sees your struggles, holds your hand, and stays.

My friend’s point was that yes, it’s good to lock in, but at some point the loneliness creeps in. You start wanting to be around someone. You want to go out, you want to share things. And imagine going through all that locking in, and at the end of it there’s nobody to share it with. That’s painful in itself. Rilke said, “***Love is two solitudes that protect and border and greet each other.***” Two solitudes. Not one. And the Bible says it straight: *“**It is not good that the man should be alone**.”*

So we agreed. Companionship is **necessary**.

My issue is that I think this generation is scared of depth.

Everything is surface level now. Social media has tricked us into thinking there are a thousand options out there, a thousand people to choose from. So when things get hard, we just move on, because there’s always someone else. People are replaceable, right?

I asked my friend: Are they, though? Really?

Because I don’t think so. Some people you just can’t replace, no matter how hard you try. You tell yourself, “I’ll find this again,” and you don’t, because what they gave you was different. What they offered was never going to come from anybody else. So I keep wondering whether we’re chasing “better,” or whether there even is a better. My friend couldn’t answer that, and I’m not going to lie, neither can I.

Which leads to my real question: why don’t we fight for these people?

None of it was ever going to be easy. Platonic, romantic, any of it. You have to fight to stay in somebody’s life. But now everyone’s overthinking everything. Am I texting too much? Am I disturbing them? Am I too much? So we pull back, and one week we’re inseparable, and the next week we’re strangers. Back and forth, back and forth.

And relationships have gone transactional. Yes, relationships have always had some give and take. But now people walk in thinking, “***What can this person do for me?***” Instead of, “***How can this person and I experience life together?***” Be fun, be real, grow.

This is also why guarding your heart matters. We live in a **wicked** world, in quotes, and a lot of people are broken. Avoidant, detached, never confronted their emotions. And whether we like it or not, the people we let in shape who we become.

That’s why I could never get **hookup** culture. How are you with one person this week and someone else next week? And when people say “it’s not that deep, it was just sex,” I pause. I actually look at the person and think, what do you mean? I hate to be the guy who brings Christianity into everything, but in the Bible that’s the highest form of intimacy. So how is it nothing?

I think that’s why so many of us end up empty. You’ve given yourself to so many people who weren’t worth it, and one day you’re sitting alone, confused, wondering why you feel so lost. You gave yourself away to people who didn’t deserve you.

And look, I’ll always be a hopeless romantic. I’ll always look for the best in people. But I think we’ve bastardized love in this generation. We think it’s butterflies. It’s not. Love is choosing someone, over and over, no matter what. *“**Love bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.**”* Whether they’re sick, broke, sad, or the ugliest person in the room that day. It’s looking at them and saying, *I wouldn’t have you any other way.*

That’s the love story I’m hoping for.

So lock in. Love yourself first. Know what you want, so you don’t waste your heart on people who were never meant to see 10% of you.

But after that, don’t stay locked in. Let somebody in. And when you find the right one, fight for them.

So what is companionship to you? I’d genuinely love to know.
